Military Branches Overview

A simple look at each branch so you know what they do, the kinds of jobs available, and how some skills can help after you get out. Always talk to a recruiter for current details.

Independent information only. Not official. Pay, bonuses, and job availability change. Confirm everything with a recruiter.

U.S. Army

What they do: Land combat and ground operations. Largest branch with the widest range of jobs (infantry, mechanics, medics, intelligence, cyber, and more).

Typical AFQT minimum: 31 with a high school diploma.

Example jobs & civilian path: Wheeled vehicle mechanic → civilian auto or diesel technician. Combat medic → EMT or healthcare support. Intelligence analyst → civilian intelligence or security roles. Many technical jobs translate to solid civilian pay after service.

U.S. Navy

What they do: Sea power, ships, submarines, and naval aviation. Strong technical training (especially nuclear and electronics).

Typical AFQT minimum: Around 35.

Example jobs & civilian path: Nuclear technician or electrician → high-paying civilian power plant, shipyard, or industrial roles. Aviation maintenance → airline or aviation technician jobs. Many technical ratings lead to well-paid civilian careers.

U.S. Air Force

What they do: Air power, aircraft, and support missions. Often noted for quality of life and technical training.

Typical AFQT minimum: Around 36.

Example jobs & civilian path: Aircraft maintenance → civilian aviation technician. Cyber or intelligence → high-demand civilian IT and security jobs. Technical skills often translate to good civilian salaries.

U.S. Marine Corps

What they do: Rapid-response amphibious force. Known for demanding training and warrior culture. Part of the Department of the Navy.

Typical AFQT minimum: Around 32.

Example jobs & civilian path: Infantry and combat roles build leadership and discipline valued by employers. Technical MOS (communications, maintenance) can lead to civilian technical or security work.

U.S. Coast Guard

What they do: Maritime law enforcement, search and rescue, port security, and environmental protection. Both a military branch and a law enforcement agency. Mostly domestic operations.

Typical AFQT minimum: Around 40 (often higher competition).

Example jobs & civilian path: Boatswain’s mate, machinery technician, or law enforcement roles can lead to civilian maritime, law enforcement, or skilled trade jobs. Many skills transfer directly to civilian maritime and safety careers.

U.S. Space Force

What they do: Space operations, satellites, and space domain awareness. Newest branch. Highly technical and selective.

Typical AFQT minimum: Around 36.

Example jobs & civilian path: Space systems operations and cyber roles translate to civilian aerospace, satellite, and high-tech jobs that often pay well.

After You Get Out

Many military jobs teach skills that employers want. Technical roles (mechanics, electronics, medical, cyber, nuclear) often lead to civilian jobs with competitive pay. Leadership experience from any branch is valued. Use your time in service to gain certifications and clear job experience. Talk to a recruiter and check current civilian job outlooks for the specific MOS or rating you are considering.

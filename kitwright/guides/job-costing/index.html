How to cost a job before you quote it

A plain way to turn materials, hours, and markup into a price you can defend.

A quote that feels right and loses money usually skipped one cost. Write the job down before you say a number: materials, your hours, what an hour costs you, subcontractors, and the small extras (the dump, the permit, the trip).

Hours times your labor rate is labor cost. Add the other lines and you have the cost of the job. Markup is what you add on top of that cost. A 40% markup on $1,100 of cost is a $1,540 price. Margin is different: profit divided by the price, which is about 28.6% in that example. People mix those two words up and underprice the work.

Use your own labor rate, not the rate you wish you charged. If a helper is on the job, put their cost in subcontractors or labor, not in the markup.

The sheet flags a margin under 15% so a thin job is obvious. That flag is a warning, not a rule. Some jobs are worth doing thin. Most are not.
